\chapter{Pictures and video}
\label{ch:stage}

Picture and video cues appear in a separate stage window, which opens beside
the main window whenever the show contains such a cue, as the storm show
does.  Move the stage window to the audience's display, such as a projector.
It stays black until a picture appears.

\section{The second act}

\begin{enumerate}
\item In the storm show, click \ui{Act 2} under \ui{Cue lists} in the
  sidebar.  The cue list shows the second act, with cue~9, Title card, on
  standby.
\item Press \ui{GO}\@.  The stage window becomes fullscreen on its display,
  comes to the front, and shows the title card, as in
  Figure~\ref{fig:stage}.  Figure~\ref{fig:showing} shows the main window at
  the same time.
\item Press \ui{GO} again.  The opening film starts over the title card and
  immediately continues to cue~11, which fades out the title card beneath
  the film so that it does not reappear when the film ends.
\item Press \ui{Panic}.  The film fades to black.
\end{enumerate}

A picture cue shows its picture until something stops it, so the next cue
always waits for GO, even if the picture cue is set to auto-follow.  A video
cue plays its picture along with its sound, and its start, end, loop, and
level work as they do for an audio cue.  A picture is shown whole, as large
as the window allows.  A new picture covers those of earlier cues, which
reappear once it is gone, and a fade cue that stops a picture or video fades
it out, as Panic does.

\key{F11} switches the stage window between fullscreen and windowed until the
next picture or video cue fires.  Closing the stage window hides it until a
picture or video cue fires again; closing the main window quits cinq.  GO and
Panic work even while the stage window has the keyboard focus.

\section*{Exercise}

You can now put pictures and films on stage.  In your own show, add a picture
cue for \texttt{pictures/title.png}, followed by a fade cue that takes it
out.
